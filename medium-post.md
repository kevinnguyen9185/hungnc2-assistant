# I Built an AI Team That Works for Me — for the Price of One Receptionist

*After a decade of building AI for other people, I finally became my own customer.*

---

I've spent more than ten years building AI systems for other people to use. Then our CEO's "AI Native" mindset finally got under my skin, and I realized something slightly embarrassing: the cobbler's children had no shoes. So I built an AI "team" whose only job is to… run my errands.

To me, AI Native isn't waiting for the company to hand you a tool. It's living with AI every day. So I started with the most honest test subject available: my own work.

## The setup sounds fancy. Using it is not.

The stack: **OpenClaw + Claude + Codex + Notion**, self-hosted on my own server.

The daily experience: I send one message via Telegram or Zalo — a voice note if I'm too lazy to type — and the "switchboard" routes the work to the right assistant.

## Meet the team

- **A DeepSeek helper** handles everyday Q&A and small chores.
- **Claude is the lead dev** — coding, exploring, writing docs.
- **Codex runs tasks in parallel**, so nobody waits for anybody.
- **Need more headcount?** Copilot, Antigravity, whatever comes next — plug it in via ACP and it starts the same day. No interviews, no onboarding buddy.

## The parts I love the most

**They do their own paperwork.** When an assistant finishes something, it creates the task on Notion, marks it Done, and commits code under its own name. I read the git log over coffee and know exactly who did what. We have never held a standup meeting.

**The assistant reminds the boss — not the other way around.** Every day at 9am, it pings me with whatever is due today. My job is to read one short message and feel mildly productive before I've done anything.

**One brain, every device.** All context lives in a shared "brain" synced via GitHub. When I open my laptop at night, Claude Desktop reads the brain and picks up exactly where the morning conversation left off. I never repeat myself. My assistants have better memory than I do, which I've decided not to take personally.

**The bill is almost insulting.** I only pay a small "receptionist" fee to keep OpenClaw at the front desk. All the heavy lifting goes to Claude and Codex — both already covered by subscriptions I was paying for anyway. Total extra cost: roughly one coffee habit.

## Was it worth it?

Building AI for others pays the bills. Building AI for yourself buys you an extra 15 minutes of sleep every morning.

Worth it.
